The Smart Buildings Control Suite

A Diverse Open Source Benchmark to Evaluate and Scale HVAC Control Policies for Sustainability

Abstract

Commercial buildings account for 17% of U.S. carbon emissions, with roughly half of that from Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning (HVAC). HVAC devices form a complex thermodynamic system, and while model predictive control and reinforcement learning have been used to optimize control policies, scaling to thousands of buildings remains a significant unsolved challenge. Most current approaches are over-optimized for specific buildings and rely on proprietary data or hard-to-configure simulators. We present the Smart Buildings Control Suite, the first open source interactive HVAC control benchmark with a focus on solutions that generalize across buildings. It has 3 components: real-world data from 11 buildings over 6 years, a lightweight data-driven simulator, and a modular Physically Informed Neural Network (PINN) building model. The buildings span multiple climates, management systems, and sizes, and both the simulator and PINN easily transfer to new buildings, ensuring solutions using this benchmark are robust to these factors and only reliant on fully scalable building models. This represents a major step towards scaling HVAC optimization from the lab to buildings everywhere. To facilitate use, our benchmark is compatible with the Gym standard, and our data is on Hugging Face.

What's in the suite

Real-world data

Observations, actions and rewards from 11 commercial buildings in California, New York and Massachusetts, in a single RL-ready format.

Calibrated simulator

A lightweight, GPU-accelerated finite-difference thermal simulator. Configured from floor plans in under 3 hours and calibrated to real data (0.57 °C mean error over a 24-hour validation day).

PINN building model

A modular, physics-informed neural network that respects physical laws, e.g. more cooling always lowers temperature. 0.3 °C MAE on 24-hour predictions.

Everything is compatible with the OpenAI Gym standard, so agents can be trained offline on real data or interactively in the simulator. A floorplan generator produces unlimited synthetic buildings for testing how policies transfer to buildings they have never seen.

Data & documentation

The real-world data from all 11 buildings is on Hugging Face under a CC BY 4.0 license. Every building is stored at its native time resolution with values exactly as recorded, together with a channel table that describes every sensor and setpoint in one vocabulary across buildings. The dataset card documents the format, the per-building caveats and the provenance of every file. 

from datasets import load_dataset
points = load_dataset("Jgoldfeder/smart-buildings", "points", split="train").to_pandas()   # channel table
sb1    = load_dataset("Jgoldfeder/smart-buildings", "SB1_observations", split="train")      # one building

The simulator, environments and RL tooling are documented at google.github.io/sbsim (setup, API reference, contributing).

Initial live trials

Two trials (5 and 11 days) of a learned control agent in a real building, compared against baseline control.

Documentation

Simulator and RL environment documentation: installation, API reference and contribution guide.

Transfer across generated buildings

A floorplan generator produces unlimited synthetic buildings, each with its own number of floors, ACs and boilers, and HVAC layout. We simulated 18 generated buildings (plus 3 reference offices) under 6 weather scenarios, from cold winter to tropical, and compared three baseline policies (all off, all on, and bang-bang) to show how the simulator behaves under extreme variation.
